# Itinerary

**26 Sept** — Two people land in Kathmandu. Hotel for two, one night. Exchange currency, rent gear, and buy Kathmandu → Pokhara night-bus tickets for the **whole group** for the 27th night. The rest of the group arrives the next afternoon, so don’t leave the tickets until then.

**27 Sept** — Remaining group lands Kathmandu in the afternoon. Whole group takes the night bus to Pokhara.

**28 Sept** — Arrive Pokhara. Mild sightseeing (Phewa Lake). Go **direct to Ghandruk** — you are not staying in a Pokhara hotel. Rest.

**29 Sept** — Trek begins from Ghandruk. After that, the trail plan is unchanged.

---

## 1. Before leaving Bangladesh

### Documents

- [ ]  Passport — check that it has sufficient validity and blank pages.
- [ ]  Nepal visa/visa-on-arrival eligibility — verify the current rule for Bangladeshi passport holders before departure.
- [ ]  Flight tickets — Dhaka ↔︎ Kathmandu (two people 26 Sept, rest 27 Sept).
- [ ]  Keep digital + offline copies of:
    - Passport
    - Flight tickets
    - Hotel bookings
    - Emergency contacts
- [ ]  Give one trusted person at home the complete itinerary and everyone’s contact numbers.

### Money

Don’t depend entirely on cards.

I’d take a combination of:

- NPR cash for the trek
- International card for Kathmandu/Pokhara
- Some emergency USD if convenient

For a group of 7–8, **one person should maintain a shared expense ledger**. Otherwise you’ll spend half the trip figuring out who paid for which jeep.

# 2. Transport — book / buy these in time

---

### Kathmandu hotel (26 Sept, two people)

The two who land on the 26th need a hotel that night. Book it before you leave Bangladesh. The rest of the group does not need a Kathmandu hotel: they land on the 27th afternoon and go straight onto the night bus.

- [ ]  Kathmandu hotel for two, 26 Sept night.

### Kathmandu → Pokhara

Night bus on **27 Sept**, whole group together.

The two who land on the 26th should buy the tickets that day. Don’t wait until the rest of the group lands on the 27th afternoon and hope there are 7–8 seats left.

- [ ]  Buy 7–8 seats together on the 27 Sept night bus.
- [ ]  Confirm the departure location.
- [ ]  Confirm the pickup time.
- [ ]  Confirm luggage allowance.
- [ ]  Keep the operator’s phone number.

You are **not** booking a Pokhara hotel. After the bus, it’s a short look at the lake and then a jeep to Ghandruk.

# 3. 26 Sept — Kathmandu (advance party of two)

This is not a sightseeing day. The two who land early are doing the group’s errands so the 27th can be a clean join-and-go.

### First priority

- [ ]  Check into the hotel (two people, one night).
- [ ]  Exchange currency / withdraw enough NPR.
- [ ]  Rent trekking equipment.
- [ ]  Buy remaining supplies.
- [ ]  Buy Kathmandu → Pokhara night-bus tickets for the whole group, 27 Sept night.
- [ ]  Confirm / start permits and trek registration if you can do them in Kathmandu.
- [ ]  Arrange the Pokhara → Ghandruk jeep for the 28th.
- [ ]  Charge everything.

### Gear rental

You planned to rent:

- Down jacket
- Trekking poles
- Sleeping bag

Before accepting rental gear:

**Actually inspect it.**

Don’t just take a bag because the shop says it’s suitable for ABC.

# 4. 27 Sept — group joins, night bus

- [ ]  Remaining group lands Kathmandu in the afternoon.
- [ ]  Everyone on the same 27 Sept night bus to Pokhara.
- [ ]  Confirm tickets, passports, and meeting point before you leave for the bus.

# 5. 28 Sept — Pokhara → Ghandruk (no Pokhara hotel)

You are not staying in Pokhara. After a mild look at the lake, go **direct to Ghandruk** and rest. Trek starts the next morning, **29 Sept**, from Ghandruk.

For 7–8 people:

**Arrange the jeep before the 28th.** Don’t land in Pokhara and start hunting for a vehicle.

Your target:

**Pokhara → Ghandruk**

Confirm:

- [ ]  Vehicle capacity
- [ ]  Number of passengers
- [ ]  Luggage capacity
- [ ]  Total price
- [ ]  Pickup location
- [ ]  Pickup time
- [ ]  Drop-off: Ghandruk
- [ ]  Whether the price includes road/parking charges

And don’t let the driver convince you to change the plan unnecessarily because some other road is “better.”

That day:

- [ ]  Arrive Pokhara (morning, after the night bus).
- [ ]  Mild sightseeing: Phewa Lake. Don’t turn it into a full day.
- [ ]  Jeep to Ghandruk.
- [ ]  Check into a Ghandruk teahouse.
- [ ]  Rest. Trek starts 29 Sept from Ghandruk.

# 7. What to physically carry

## Clothing

Don’t overpack.

### Upper body

- [ ]  2–3 quick-dry T-shirts
- [ ]  1–2 thermal/base layers
- [ ]  Fleece/mid-layer
- [ ]  Down jacket
- [ ]  Waterproof rain jacket
- [ ]  Lightweight windbreaker if your rain jacket isn’t suitable as a wind shell

### Lower body

- [ ]  2 trekking pants
- [ ]  1 thermal bottom
- [ ]  Comfortable underwear
- [ ]  Warm sleeping clothes

### Extremities

These matter more than people expect:

- [ ]  3–4 pairs trekking socks
- [ ]  Warm wool/synthetic socks
- [ ]  Warm gloves
- [ ]  Lightweight liner gloves
- [ ]  Warm beanie
- [ ]  Sun hat/cap
- [ ]  Buff/neck gaiter

---

# 8. Footwear

Your boots are important. **Do not arrive in Nepal with brand-new boots.**

Before the trip:

- Wear them regularly.
- Walk long distances.
- Walk stairs.
- Make sure your heel isn’t slipping.
- Identify pressure points.
- Break them in properly.

Take:

- [ ]  Trekking boots
- [ ]  Extra socks

I’d also carry **blister treatment** rather than assuming you won’t need it.

---

# 9. Rain protection

September is still close to the monsoon season.

This deserves emphasis.

Keep your rain gear **inside your daypack**, not buried in your luggage.

Take:

- [ ]  Waterproof rain jacket
- [ ]  Backpack rain cover
- [ ]  Dry bags / waterproof bags
- [ ]  Ziplock bags

And use waterproof bags for:

- Passport
- Phone
- Power bank
- Wallet
- Electronics
- Extra clothes

A cheap plastic bag can save your electronics.

---

# 10. Food/snacks

You don’t need to carry food for six days. The teahouses feed you.

But carry **high-energy emergency snacks**.

I’d take:

- [ ]  Chocolate
- [ ]  Dates
- [ ]  Nuts
- [ ]  Dry fruits
- [ ]  Energy bars
- [ ]  Biscuits
- [ ]  Electrolyte/ORS packets
- [ ]  Some salty snacks

Your idea of carrying **saline/electrolytes** is good. Don’t rely on sugary snacks alone. Having something salty can be surprisingly useful.

---

# 11. Water

Carry a reusable bottle/hydration system.

I’d aim for roughly **2 litres of carrying capacity per person**.

You can refill along the trail.

Take one of:

- Water purification tablets
- Water purification drops
- A suitable filter

Don’t assume every water source is automatically safe just because locals drink from it.

---

# 12. Medicine / first aid

Have a **small group first-aid kit**, rather than eight people carrying eight full kits.

Include things such as:

- [ ]  Bandages
- [ ]  Blister patches
- [ ]  Antiseptic
- [ ]  Painkiller
- [ ]  Basic stomach/diarrhea medicine
- [ ]  ORS/electrolytes
- [ ]  Motion-sickness medication if anyone needs it
- [ ]  Personal prescription medicines
- [ ]  Sunscreen
- [ ]  Lip balm with SPF

### Altitude

This is more important than having a huge medicine kit. At ABC you’re around **4,130 m**. Watch for:

- Persistent headache
- Nausea
- Dizziness
- Unusual fatigue
- Difficulty sleeping
- Loss of coordination
- Shortness of breath at rest

Don’t treat worsening altitude symptoms as something to “push through.” **Descending is the treatment for serious altitude illness.**

---

# 13. Sun protection

This gets underestimated.

At altitude, UV exposure can be intense.

- [ ]  Sunglasses — preferably good UV protection
- [ ]  Sunscreen
- [ ]  Lip balm
- [ ]  Cap/hat

I’d actually consider sunglasses **essential**, not optional.

---

# 14. Electronics

- [ ]  Phone
- [ ]  Power bank
- [ ]  Charging cables
- [ ]  Universal travel adapter
- [ ]  Headlamp
- [ ]  Spare batteries if your headlamp uses them

### Headlamp

Don’t rely on your phone flashlight. You’ll potentially be walking before sunrise or after dark if something goes wrong. Each person should ideally have their **own headlamp**.

---

# 15. Offline navigation

Do this in Kathmandu on the 26th, or on the 28th **before** the jeep. You are not staying in Pokhara, so don’t plan to sit in a hotel room and download maps.

- [ ]  Download offline map of the entire ABC route.
- [ ]  Save your teahouse destinations.
- [ ]  Save Ghandruk (first night, 28 Sept).
- [ ]  Save jeep pickup/drop-off locations.

Don’t assume you’ll have reliable mobile data everywhere.

---

# 16. Teahouse expectations

This isn’t a hotel trip. Expect:

- Basic rooms
- Often shared bathrooms
- Hot showers sometimes available for extra money
- Charging sometimes available for extra money
- Wi-Fi sometimes available, sometimes poor
- Cold rooms at higher elevations

At higher elevations, **the dining room is often the warmest place** because there’s a stove.

---

# 17. Cash

This is one of the most important practical things. Carry enough **Nepali rupees** for the entire trek plus emergency money.

Don’t assume: “*I’ll just withdraw money when I need it.*”

ATM availability becomes increasingly irrelevant once you’re deep into the trail. Also keep emergency cash separated from your main wallet.

---

# 18. Group management

Most important rule: **Nobody walks alone**, sspecially above Deurali.

If someone is slower, the group should adapt rather than splitting into random individuals.

---

# 19. Daily trekking routine

I’d establish this routine before starting:

**6:00–6:30**
Wake up

**6:30–7:00**
Breakfast

**7:00–8:00**
Start walking

**Every ~60–90 min**
Short break

**Lunch**
Around midday

**Afternoon**
Reach destination early enough to get a room

**Evening**
Eat, shower if possible, charge devices, prepare backpack

**Sleep early**

Don’t turn the teahouse into a late-night hangout. Your next morning matters more.

---

# 20. One particularly important thing: don’t chase the itinerary

You have a planned schedule. That’s good. But the mountains don’t care about your schedule.

If:

- Someone becomes sick
- Weather becomes dangerous
- A road is blocked
- The group is exhausted
- A trail condition becomes bad

**Change the plan.**

The goal is not: “We must reach ABC on Day 6.”

The goal is: “Everyone reaches ABC safely.”
